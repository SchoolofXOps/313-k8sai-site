Lab — Queues, Gang Admission, Device Accounting and Topology on a Simulated Fleet

Lab Truth Contract — SIMULATED

The control plane, the kube-scheduler's binding decisions, and Kueue's admission, preemption and accounting are real Kubernetes behaviour on a real kind cluster. The fleet's kubelets are simulated: the nine extra nodes are kwok nodes, so no container runs on them and nothing is timed or sized there. The devices are synthetic, published by the upstream example driver on the one real worker. There is no accelerator anywhere in this lab, and nothing here measures one.

In this lab you take the grid-core cluster, add the synthetic-device layer from Module 4, and attach a simulated fleet of nine nodes run by Kueue's queueing layer. Then you play the platform team for a company with three teams, research, data and product, who all want the same capacity. Every part ends with something going wrong on purpose, and you read each failure from the cluster's own objects: a condition and its reason, an admission field, an event, a .spec.nodeName. You never read it from a Pod's phase alone.

Think of this lab as running the booking desk of a shared office building. Each team has a booked number of desks. A team can borrow desks another team is not using, and has to give them back when the owner returns. Some teams must sit together in one room or not at all. And the building's fire marshal has a head count of their own, which the booking desk never sees. Every part below is one of those rules, or one of the ways they fight.

Run every command from the root of your clone of the labs repository, as in the earlier modules. If you open a second terminal, export KUBECONFIG there again, and re-run the block that defines the lab's shell functions (you will meet it at the start of part 0 and part 3).

Where the platform stands​

Think of where the platform is as a building that now has rooms with labelled equipment, and no booking desk.

After Module 4 the platform can address a synthetic device by attribute and read a device's health from a Pod's status, and the cluster is torn down. Your repository holds the node layer under grid-platform/nodes/ and the wrappers for the synthetic-device layer under grid-platform/clusters/addon-dra/.

What is still broken is the thing Module 4 ended on: nothing stops one tenant from taking every device or every node, and a group of Pods can still be half-placed. This lab adds quota, queues, gang admission, preemption and topology, and when you finish, the platform can hold three tenants to a fair share. Your repository fills grid-platform/capacity/: the flavours, the queues, the priority classes, the topology, the queueing controller's values, its gate read-back and your own device-accounting record. The data and network paths will start starving this fair pool in Module 6.

Two kinds share one name​

The grid-core profile turns on the core GenericWorkload gate, and the queueing layer installs Kueue. Each brings a kind called Workload: the core Workload in scheduling.k8s.io and the Kueue Workload in kueue.x-k8s.io. Imagine two colleagues in one office who are both called Sam. Shout "Sam!" across the room and one of them answers, and it may not be the one you wanted. This part makes you hear the wrong Sam answer.

First, the lab's shell functions. Four reads repeat all through this page, so they are written once here. m5_wl prints one line per Kueue Workload in a namespace: who owns it, whether it is admitted, on which flavour, and the reason on its QuotaReserved condition. It reads through the full resource name, workloads.kueue.x-k8s.io, and prints fields, never the kind's bare name. m5_bound counts a group's Pods and how many of them have a node. m5_wait_admitted waits until a number of Kueue Workloads in a namespace are admitted. m5_wait_bound waits until a number of a group's Pods have a node.

m5_wl() {
  kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n "$1" get workloads.kueue.x-k8s.io -o json | jq -r '.items[]
    | "owner=" + ((.metadata.ownerReferences // []) | map(.name) | first // "none")
    + " admitted=" + (if .status.admission == null then "no" else "yes" end)
    + " flavor=" + ((.status.admission.podSetAssignments[0].flavors.cpu) // "none")
    + " qr=" + ([.status.conditions[]? | select(.type == "QuotaReserved") | .reason] | first // "none")'
}
m5_bound() {
  kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n "$1" get pods -l app="$2" -o json \
    | jq -r --arg a "$2" '"\($a): pods=" + (.items | length | tostring) + " bound=" + ([.items[] | select(.spec.nodeName != null)] | length | tostring)'
}
m5_wait_admitted() {
  for i in $(seq 1 60); do
    n=$(kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n "$1" get workloads.kueue.x-k8s.io -o json | jq '[.items[] | select(.status.admission != null)] | length')
    [ "$n" -ge "$2" ] && break
    sleep 2
  done
  echo "admitted in $1: $n"
}
m5_wait_bound() {
  for i in $(seq 1 60); do
    n=$(kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n "$1" get pods -l app="$2" -o json | jq '[.items[] | select(.spec.nodeName != null)] | length')
    [ "$n" -ge "$3" ] && break
    sleep 2
  done
  m5_bound "$1" "$2"
}
for f in m5_wl m5_bound m5_wait_admitted m5_wait_bound; do command -v "$f" >/dev/null && echo "defined: $f"; done

Device accounting against quota​

This part answers one question: when a workload claims a synthetic device, is that device counted against quota? Think of a hotel minibar. If the minibar has no price list, the hotel cannot charge you for what you take, and a well-run hotel does not just let you take it free: it locks the minibar until the price list is fixed. Kueue's price list is its device-class mapping: a line in its own configuration that says "a claim for DeviceClass sim-accelerator.example.com counts as one unit of example.com/sim-accelerator". You will switch Kueue's DRA integration off, then put the defaults back, then remove the price list, and read the result on the Kueue Workload each time.

The device workload gets its own ClusterQueue, devices-cq, whose only flavour is the real worker and whose device quota is 0. There is a reason for that. With the integration switched off, Kueue cannot see the device at all, so a tenant queue with two flavours would send the workload to the first flavour with cpu room, the simulated fleet, where no driver publishes any device and the claim could never be allocated. The device has to land on the one node with a kubelet and a driver, so its queue offers only that node.

The workload itself is a Job, not a StatefulSet: a suspended Job creates no Pod until it is admitted, so "no Pod" means exactly that. It runs on the real worker, where kwok's job-completion shortcut does not reach, so its Pod stays running and keeps its device. Its Pod claims one device through the template one-sim-accelerator, which names the class and nothing else.

Five helpers do the mechanical work, so each posture below is a few short lines. m5_apply_config writes a file into the ConfigMap key. m5_mapping reads the live key back and runs the queueing layer's own probe on it, which reports the mapping. m5_restart restarts the controller and then waits for three things, in order: the rollout, the webhook's endpoints naming only the new controller Pod, and a server-side dry run of a probe Job succeeding. That last wait matters. Kueue answers a webhook on every Job you create, and for a few seconds after a restart nothing answers it, so an apply in that window fails with failed calling webhook. The dry run sends m5/webhook-probe.yaml through the webhooks and stores nothing. m5_gates reads the new controller Pod's own Loaded feature gates log line and prints only the KueueDRA gates. m5_wait_wl waits until Kueue has created the Kueue Workload for a Job you just submitted. Right after a restart that takes a moment: the new controller Pod answers the webhook before it holds the leader lease that lets it create anything, and a kubectl wait on a Kueue Workload that does not exist yet fails at once with no matching resources found.

m5_apply_config() {
  kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n kueue-system patch cm kueue-manager-config --type=merge \
    -p "$(jq -n --rawfile c "$1" '{data: {"controller_manager_config.yaml": $c}}')"
}
m5_mapping() {
  kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n kueue-system get cm kueue-manager-config -o json \
    | jq -j '.data["controller_manager_config.yaml"]' > "$M5_SCRATCH/live.yaml"
  node clusters/grid-core/addons/kueue-kwok/kueue-config-probe.mjs "$M5_SCRATCH/live.yaml" sim-accelerator.example.com
}
m5_restart() {
  kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n kueue-system rollout restart deploy/kueue-controller-manager
  kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n kueue-system rollout status deploy/kueue-controller-manager --timeout=300s
  NEW_POD=$(kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n kueue-system get pods -l control-plane=controller-manager -o json \
    | jq -r '[.items[] | select(.metadata.deletionTimestamp == null)] | sort_by(.metadata.creationTimestamp) | last | .metadata.name')
  for i in $(seq 1 60); do
    EP=$(kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n kueue-system get endpointslices -l kubernetes.io/service-name=kueue-webhook-service -o json \
      | jq -r '[.items[].endpoints[]? | select(.conditions.ready == true) | .targetRef.name] | sort | join(",")')
    [ "$EP" = "$NEW_POD" ] && break
    sleep 3
  done
  DRY=failed
  for i in $(seq 1 60); do
    if kubectl --context kind-grid-core create --dry-run=server -f m5/webhook-probe.yaml -o name >/dev/null 2>&1; then DRY=ok; break; fi
    sleep 2
  done
  echo "new controller Pod: $NEW_POD webhook endpoints: $EP dry-run: $DRY"
}
m5_gates() {
  kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n kueue-system logs "$NEW_POD" -c manager | grep -F 'Loaded feature gates' | tail -n 1 \
    | grep -oE '"KueueDRA[A-Za-z]*"[[:space:]]*:[[:space:]]*(true|false)' | tr -d ' "' | sort | tr '\n' ' '
  echo
}
m5_wait_wl() {
  for i in $(seq 1 60); do
    n=$(kubectl --context kind-grid-core -n m5-devices get workloads.kueue.x-k8s.io -o json \
      | jq --arg j "$1" '[.items[] | select(any(.metadata.ownerReferences[]?; .name == $j))] | length')
    [ "$n" -ge 1 ] && break
    sleep 2
  done
  echo "Kueue Workloads for $1: $n"
}

The before state: the integration switched off​

Switching Kueue's DRA integration off takes four gates, set false together, in the configuration's featureGates: map: KueueDRAIntegration, KueueDRAIntegrationExtendedResource, KueueDRAIntegrationPartitionableDevices and KueueDRARejectWorkloadsWhenDRADisabled. Two quotas that disagree​

The building's fire marshal has their own head count, and the booking desk never sees it. In Kubernetes the fire marshal is a namespace ResourceQuota: the API server checks it every time a Pod is created. Kueue checks its own quota once, when it admits a group. Here the two disagree.

m5/quota-incident.yaml adds a ResourceQuota, product-quota, that allows 8 cpu of requests in product. product-serve's sixteen Pods already hold 8 cpu, and product-ghost's one Pod, still waiting for a node, holds another 500m, so the quota reads 8500m used against 8 allowed. The namespace has no cpu left, while product-cq still has room. Then it adds a one-Pod Job, quota-collision, through product-queue. It is a Job, never a StatefulSet, on purpose: a StatefulSet's Pods are created before Kueue decides anything, so the namespace quota would refuse them first, and you would never see Kueue admit. A suspended Job creates no Pod until it is admitted, so the order of events is the order you would meet in a real incident. Events expire after about an hour, so read this part in the same sitting as the rest of the lab.

kubectl --context kind-grid-core apply -f m5/quota-incident.yaml

Known failures and their fixes​

Each entry below is something that actually happened in one of the course's own runs of this page, with the step it happened in named, so you can compare it with your own output.

The failures this page causes on purpose are not listed here: the short form of the kind name that finds nothing, the stranded eighth Pod, the starved tenant, the flavour no node matches, the unmapped device class and the Job the namespace quota refuses. Two of the entries come from the course's first run of this page, before the page was fixed, so you will meet them only if your clone is older than the fix or you leave out a step the page now runs. The other two are things every run prints.

The layer's verify prints FAIL: zero Kueue Workloads on 'grid-core', and PROFILE.md has no OK: line. Seen in the course's first run, when the queueing layer's verify ran straight after the layer was created, before part 0's probe. Applied with TAS_GANGS=none, the layer submits nothing of its own, and its verify refuses to report clean while Kueue has admitted nothing. The page pipes the verify into tee, and the tee hides the verify's exit code, so the step looks fine until you read it. The page now runs the verify after the collision probe is admitted. If you see this line, run the probe block, wait for admitted in m5-collision: 1, and then run the verify and the profile record again.

kubectl wait prints error: no matching resources found right after a restart, and the projection under it prints nothing. Seen in the course's first run, at the after-state step, which submits devices-over-quota under the restored defaults. Think of a shop that has unlocked its door but whose cashier has not arrived yet: you can walk in, and nobody rings you up. The new controller Pod answers the webhook a moment before it holds the leader lease that lets it create the Kueue Workload, so the wait found nothing to wait on, and the accounting record's mapped-defaults: line came out empty. The page now runs m5_wait_wl devices-over-quota before each of those waits, and in the course's second run it printed Kueue Workloads for devices-over-quota: 1 every time. If your record's mapped-defaults: line is empty, run the after-state step again, then the record step.

The topology negative control reads WaitingForQuota, not a topology reason. Seen in both of the course's runs, at the block-gang-refused step. It is not a failure. At the pinned Kueue release, a group that waits for a topology domain to free up carries the same reason as a group that is short of quota. The message after the first colon is what names the topology: topology "northwind-topology" allows to fit only 2 out of 4 pod(s). The course's first run printed only the reason, which is why the page now prints the message as well.

rollout status prints 1 old replicas are pending termination... during every controller restart. Seen at all four restarts in both of the course's runs, in part 3. It is not a failure: the old controller Pod is still shutting down while the new one starts, and the next line, successfully rolled out, is the one that counts. m5_restart then waits for the webhook and the dry run before it hands back to you.

If a step fails in a way the page does not describe, read the object that step reads, in the form the page reads it, before you re-run anything. Most failures on this page are a Kueue Workload whose QuotaReserved condition says why, or a Pod whose PodScheduled condition does.

Where you will use this​

When a training job sits Pending at work while accelerators idle, you will read its Kueue Workload's QuotaReserved reason before you blame the scheduler, and you will know whether you are looking at a quota refusal, a topology refusal or a flavour nothing matches. The next time one team starves another, you will reach for a preemption policy and a lending limit, not a bigger priority number. The next time someone says devices are "free" without a mapping, you will ask which posture their controller runs in, and read it from the controller Pod's own log. And the next time an admitted job has no Pods, you will look for a namespace ResourceQuota before you look anywhere else.
